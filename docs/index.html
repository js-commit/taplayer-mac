What it is for

Reaching for ⌘ or ⌥ means leaving the home row, and on a tablet keyboard it can mean reaching for a modifier that is not there at all. TapLayer puts the modifiers under the fingers already resting on a s d f and j k l ;. Tap one and you get the letter, exactly as before. Hold it and it becomes a modifier.

It was built for a specific frustration: typing into a Mac from an iPad or Android tablet over Jump Desktop. Those keystrokes arrive as synthetic events, which the usual remapping tools never see. TapLayer works at the layer they actually arrive on — and can remap the Mac's own keyboards too, if you want it to.

Beyond the home row

Install

  1. Open the dmg and drag TapLayer to Applications.
  2. Launch it. A ⌨ icon appears in the menu bar — there is no window and no Dock icon.
  3. Tick TapLayer in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Nothing is remapped until you do; it starts working the moment you flip the switch, no restart.
  4. Typing from a tablet? It works immediately. For the Mac's own keyboard, turn on Remap Local Keyboards in the menu.

It adds itself as a login item on first launch, so it is there after a reboot. Turn that off in the same menu.

If you change your mind mid-sentence

The kill switch is Left Ctrl + Space + Esc, which stops the engine from anywhere, including from the tablet. Quit is also always in the menu.

What it does with your keystrokes

It is a keyboard remapper, so it sees what you type. That is worth being precise about:

  • It never records what you type. The keystroke-logging diagnostics used during development are not compiled into this build — it refuses those options outright rather than merely defaulting them off.
  • It has no network code. Nothing is uploaded, because nothing can be.
  • Its log holds events, not text — connections, layer changes, errors. It is readable from the menu, kept private to your account, and capped in size.
  • Modifiers stand down in password fields. When macOS signals secure input, the remapping steps aside.
  • Accessibility is the only permission it asks for. No root, no full disk access, no screen recording.

Good to know

Fast typists roll their fingers across the home row, and a naive hold-for-modifier scheme turns that into garbage. TapLayer waits out a short hold, watches for the roll, and resolves the ambiguity before committing — so ordinary prose types normally. If a mod ever feels too eager or too slow, the timings are adjustable.

Capitalising many words in a row is the one thing that still feels slower, since every capital waits out the hold. The real ⇧ keys are still right there at your pinkies.
